Do artist influences follow the family tree of genres?

Everyone knows the family tree of popular music: blues leads to rock and roll, rock and roll leads to rock, and so on. But musicians don't read family trees. They listen to other musicians. So does influence between artists really follow the tree?

RIFF is a knowledge graph that puts both things in one place: genres and where they come from, and artists and who influenced them. Then we can compare them with SPARQL.

What we found

Of the artist pairs that cross a genre border, 66% follow the family tree (a direct link or a link through one genre in between), 4% run against it, and 29% have no link in the tree at all.

So the tree explains much of how influence moves, but not all of it. Many of the routes with no link are genres that sit close to each other and the tree just doesn't connect them. These results depend on our choice of artists and genres, so they are a starting point for discussion and not a final answer. See Method for how to read the labels and for the limits.

A second question: instruments

Do genres and influences shape which instruments artists play? For each instrument an artist plays (leaving out the voice), we check two things. Does an artist who influenced them play it too? And does an artist of one of the genres their own genre comes from play it?

Both: 57%. Only the influences: 4%. Only the genre roots: 27%. Neither: 12%.

Most instruments are shared with both, mainly guitar, bass and drums, which almost everyone plays. An instrument that only the influences explain is rare. The rows to look at are the rarer instruments in the table below, such as turntables and the drum machine, which often appear with neither.
