Ontology

The RIFF ontology extends Music Meta. Music Meta already has artists, genres, recordings and a simple "is influenced by" link. It has no way to say how we know about an influence, and it has no links between genres. RIFF adds those two things.

Namespace: https://w3id.org/riff/ontology#. Download: riff.ttl.

One example

The influence of Led Zeppelin on Nirvana, as it is in the graph. riff:Influence is the influence itself. Each riff:InfluenceClaim is what one source says about it: here one claim comes from a sentence on Wikipedia and one from Wikidata. The last part is the plain mm:isInfluencedBy link that we get from the influence.

@prefix artist: <https://w3id.org/riff/artist/> .
@prefix claim: <https://w3id.org/riff/claim/> .
@prefix influence: <https://w3id.org/riff/influence/> .
@prefix mm: <https://w3id.org/polifonia/ontology/music-meta/> .
@prefix prov: <http://www.w3.org/ns/prov#> .
@prefix riff: <https://w3id.org/riff/ontology#> .
@prefix wd: <http://www.wikidata.org/entity/> .

influence:nirvana--led_zeppelin a riff:Influence ;
    riff:influenced artist:nirvana ;
    riff:influencer artist:led_zeppelin .

claim:nirvana--led_zeppelin--attributed a riff:InfluenceClaim ;
    prov:wasDerivedFrom <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin> ;
    riff:describes influence:nirvana--led_zeppelin ;
    riff:evidenceQuote "They were also an important influence on the development of alternative rock, as bands adapted elements from the \"Zeppelin sound\" of the mid-1970s, including the Smashing Pumpkins, Nirvana, Pearl Jam, and Soundgarden."@en ;
    riff:hasEvidenceKind riff:Attributed .

claim:nirvana--led_zeppelin--unsourced a riff:InfluenceClaim ;
    prov:wasDerivedFrom wd:Q11649 ;
    riff:describes influence:nirvana--led_zeppelin ;
    riff:hasEvidenceKind riff:Unsourced .

artist:nirvana mm:isInfluencedBy artist:led_zeppelin .

Classes

TermMeaning
void:DatasetA set of RDF data. We use it to describe the graph itself.
sit:SituationThe situation pattern from ontologydesignpatterns.org: one thing that links many others.
skos:ConceptA concept in a classification scheme, from SKOS.
mm:MusicArtistA person or a group that makes music.
mm:MusicEnsenbleA band or other music group. The name is spelled this way in Music Meta.
mm:MusicGenreA category of music. Genres are individuals here, not classes, because a subgenre is not a kind of its parent in the logical sense. Subgenres use skos:broader.
mm:MusicianA single person who makes music.
mm:RecordingA recorded performance of a piece of music.
riff:EvidenceKindA way of knowing about an influence.
riff:GenreFormOne of the four forms of a music genre described by Lena and Peterson.
riff:GenrePhaseA genre in one form, at one step of its history.
riff:GenreRouteArtist influence added up from one genre to another. These are not entered by hand: query 09 builds them.
riff:InfluenceThe influence of one artist on another. This is the situation itself, so there is one for each pair of artists. What the sources say about it is kept in riff:InfluenceClaim.
riff:InfluenceClaimWhat one source says about an influence, or one recording that shows it. It has the evidence kind, the source, and the quote or the recordings. One influence can have many claims, for example one from Wikipedia and one from Wikidata.
riff:InstrumentA musical instrument, or the voice. It is close to Instrument in the Music Ontology, but that class does not include the voice.
riff:InstrumentGroupA broad group of instruments. The groups loosely follow the top level of the Hornbostel-Sachs classification (strings, winds, drums and percussion, electronic), but keyboards and the voice get their own group.
riff:OriginClaimWhat one source says about when and where a genre appeared: a range of years, a place, the source's own wording and the source. One genre can have many claims, for example one from Wikipedia and one from Wikidata. It follows the same pattern as riff:InfluenceClaim.

Object properties

TermMeaning
skos:broaderWe use it for subgenres: the subject is a subgenre of the object.
prov:wasDerivedFromThe source of a claim: a Wikipedia page, a Wikidata item or a MusicBrainz recording. Used for influence claims, origin claims and genre phases.
mm:hasGenreAn artist works in this genre. An artist can have many.
mm:isInfluencedByThe simple link between two artists. We get it from riff:Influence with a property chain. It is not transitive on purpose: if A influenced B and B influenced C, A did not have to influence C.
riff:describesThe influence that this claim is about.
riff:hasEvidenceKindWhat kind of evidence this claim is.
riff:hasFormThe form the genre has in this phase.
riff:hasPrimaryGenreThe one genre an artist is best known for. We need it to compare artists with the genre family tree.
riff:hasStylisticOriginThis genre grew out of the other genre. Not declared transitive: use the property path riff:hasStylisticOrigin+ in SPARQL to follow long chains.
riff:inInstrumentGroupThe group the instrument belongs to.
riff:inRecordingThe newer recording that uses the older one.
riff:influencedThe artist who received the influence.
riff:influencerThe artist who had the influence.
riff:isFusionOfThis genre mixes the other genre with at least one more.
riff:originOfThe genre that this claim is about.
riff:phaseOfThe genre this phase belongs to.
riff:playsInstrumentThe artist plays this instrument. For a band it means that a member played it in that band.
riff:routeFromThe genre of the artists who had the influence.
riff:routeToThe genre of the artists who received the influence.
riff:viaRecordingThe older recording that was sampled or covered.

Data properties

TermMeaning
riff:activeFromThe year the artist started.
riff:activeToThe year the artist stopped or died. Empty if still active.
riff:claimedPlaceWhere the source says the genre appeared.
riff:dateNoteExplains an influence that looks wrong by date but is correct, for example an older artist influenced by a younger one.
riff:earliestYearThe first year of the period the source gives. A period such as mid-1960s becomes 1963 to 1967 by a fixed rule, described on the method page.
riff:emergedInThe earliest year of the genre's preferred origin claim, so it means: not before this year. It is copied from riff:OriginClaim so that simple queries need no claims. Empty when the preferred source gives no date.
riff:evidenceQuoteThe sentence that states the influence.
riff:isPreferredTrue for the one claim per genre whose first year and place are copied to riff:emergedIn and riff:originPlace.
riff:latestYearThe last year of the period the source gives. The same as the earliest year when the source gives one year.
riff:originNoteExplains a claim that needs it, for example why a genre has no year.
riff:originPlaceWhere the genre first appeared, copied from its preferred origin claim. Other sources may name other places: see riff:OriginClaim.
riff:phaseOrderThe position of this phase in the genre's history, starting from 1.
riff:routeStatusHow the route compares with the genre family tree: direct, two-step, reversed or hidden.
riff:routeWeightHow many artist pairs support this route.
riff:sourceWordingWhat the source says, before it was turned into years, for example: Mid-1960s, United States.

Individuals

TermMeaning
riff:AttributedSomeone else says it, such as a critic or the article itself. The artist's own words are not reported.
riff:AvantGardeA small group of musicians trying out a new style.
riff:CoverVersionThe newer recording plays the song again. Lacasse calls this allosonic.
riff:DeclaredThe influenced artist says it. For example: she cited Mariah Carey as an early influence.
riff:DrumsAndPercussionThe drum kit and other instruments that are struck or shaken.
riff:ElectronicInstruments that make their sound electronically, such as synthesizer, drum machine, sampler and turntables.
riff:IndustryBasedThe music industry produces and sells the music.
riff:KeyboardsInstruments played from a keyboard, such as piano and organ. Synthesizers count as electronic.
riff:SamplingThe newer recording reuses the sound of the older recording. Lacasse calls this autosonic.
riff:SceneBasedA local community forms around the music.
riff:StringsInstruments with strings, such as guitar, bass guitar and violin. Electric guitars count here too.
riff:TraditionalistFans and musicians work to keep the music as it was.
riff:UnsourcedA Wikidata statement. We did not trace who made the claim.
riff:VoiceSinging and rapping.
riff:WindsAndBrassInstruments played by blowing, such as saxophone, trumpet and harmonica.
